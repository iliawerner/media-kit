03 — What the data says about you

Strengths, blind spots, and one open goal.

Insights

Every line below is a tendency observed across forty-four videos. Sample sizes are small. Read these as your own habits, not as laws.

Your strengths

  • Concrete artefacts. Every time you tie an idea to an object you can point a finger at — 0.5mm, twenty pixels, the Leopard dock, a flat edge — you win. ×2.6 on the median.
  • The Apple anchor. 60.9K median when Apple is in the title; 14.7K without. Stable across three exports. The recommendation surface trusts this combination from you.
  • Critique with specifics. “Admits Defeat”, “Death of Premium”, “Stopped Listening” — negative framing on Apple consistently lands. Not contrarianism, but informed dissent.
  • Title testing. #107 grew from 4.6K to 11.8K (+158%) after a title swap nine days post-publish. Few channels do this. You should keep doing it.

Your blind spots

  • UI elements told as origin stories. Four for four down: Door Latch, Links Are Blue, Like Button is 200K Years Old, Interface We Lost for 2000 Years. The same topic told as “here’s how it works now” would have flipped each one.
  • Concept as the main object. Reality Distortion, Aesthetics, Post-PC, Premium — abstractions without a physical anchor underperform 4 of 5 times. The one win (“Death of Premium”, 128K) succeeds because iPhone 17 Pro is the real subject.
  • Question titles without an Apple anchor. ÷2.6 on the median. “Why Does It Look So Good?”, “Why Links Are Blue” — the openers fail without a brand inside.
  • Format consistency. Five of the last six were 🟡 or 🔴. Cadence has slipped. The breakout on #109 reset the streak — don’t squander it.

Your open goal

A non-Apple video that beats the Apple median. Across the entire growth period, this has happened exactly once — “The Hidden Cost of Clean Design” (Winamp vs. Apple Music, 501K) — and even that one had Apple in the comparison. A clean non-Apple hit is the next category line you haven’t crossed.

F5 — UI element told as Story, not History

Cursor, dock, notification, badge — only works as “here’s how it works now.” As History (“here’s where it came from”), the same object loses 9× on the median. The genre is the variable.

Four questions to ask yourself before you greenlight
  1. Is there an Apple anchor — in the topic or in the title itself?
  2. Can I draw the noun in the title? If it’s an abstraction, I’m playing on hard mode.
  3. If the topic is a UI element — am I telling it as Story, not History?
  4. Is the thumbnail anchored in text and not in a physical object?
